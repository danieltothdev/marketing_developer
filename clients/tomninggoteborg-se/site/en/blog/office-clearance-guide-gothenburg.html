Office clearance in Gothenburg – a guide for companies moving or closing down

Confidentiality, IT security and reinstatement requirements make an office clearance something other than an ordinary clearance. Here is the whole process, step by step.

An office clearance in Gothenburg is normally planned two to four weeks in advance and often carried out in evenings or at weekends so the business is not disrupted. The price is fixed after an on-site inspection and depends on the size of the premises, the volume of furniture and whether the space must be reinstated to its original condition.

What separates this from a private clearance is that an office has three things that must be handled correctly: confidential documents, IT equipment holding company data, and a lease that often requires the premises to be returned in a certain state. Getting any of those wrong can be expensive. This guide covers the whole process for companies moving, downsizing or closing down.

Planning

When should planning start?

Start two to four weeks before the premises must be empty. That sounds like a lot, but in practice you need time to: terminate or renegotiate subscriptions, inventory what moves to the new premises, inform staff, book the lift and loading bay in the building, and agree with the landlord what reinstatement covers.

For larger wind-downs – a whole floor or several premises – six to eight weeks is more realistic, especially if furniture is to be resold rather than recycled. We are happy to come out for a free inspection early in the process, even if the clearance itself is far off, so you have a budget figure to work with.

The lease

Reinstatement to original condition – what does it mean?

Most commercial leases contain a reinstatement clause. It means the premises must be returned in the same condition as when you moved in, apart from normal wear and tear. In practice this usually means removing what you installed yourselves.

  • Dismantling partition walls and glass screens added later.
  • Removing signage, logos and decor from the facade, entrance and glazing.
  • Removing fitted furniture – kitchenettes, reception desks, built-in cupboards.
  • Making good holes and fixings in walls and ceilings left by shelves and screens.
  • Removing cable trays and network cabling installed for your operation.
  • Final cleaning ahead of the inspection with the landlord.

Read the lease before ordering – what counts as reinstatement varies. We walk the premises with you at the inspection and point out what is likely covered, so you can confirm with the landlord in good time. See also our office clearance service.

Documents

Confidentiality: documents and GDPR

This is where things most often go wrong. An office holds personnel files, contracts, customer registers, payslips and notes containing personal data. Throwing them in a skip is not an option – under the GDPR you remain responsible for that data until it has been securely destroyed.

The routine we recommend: sort into three piles before clearance day – to keep (move, digitise or archive as required by accounting law), to destroy securely, and contains no personal data. What is to be destroyed is handled separately through secure document destruction, not as ordinary paper waste.

Remember that accounting records have a statutory retention period – they must not be destroyed even if the business is closing. If in doubt, keep and digitise.

IT

IT equipment and data security

Computers, servers, copiers and even modern printers contain storage media with company data. Simply sending them for recycling without action is a security risk. Before equipment leaves the premises, hard drives should either be securely wiped or physically destroyed.

Bear in mind too that copiers and multifunction printers often store copies of everything scanned – this is almost always forgotten. If equipment is leased it must be returned to the lessor rather than scrapped; check the agreements before clearance day.

Working IT equipment often has a resale value. We help separate what can be sold on from what should go to electrical recycling.

Furniture

What happens to the office furniture?

Office furniture is the category with the best chance of a second life. Desks, sit-stand frames, conference furniture and task chairs from known brands often have a clear resale value – particularly if under ten years old and in good condition.

We go through the inventory with you and divide it into: sellable (can be valued and offset against the cost), donatable (schools, associations and non-profits are glad to receive working furniture), and recycling (metal, wood and textiles sorted separately). Read more about how sorting works in what happens to the items after a clearance.

Execution

Evenings, weekends and a business still running

Most office clearances we carry out in Gothenburg happen outside office hours. There are two reasons: so the business can continue undisturbed until the last day, and because lifts and loading bays are free in the evenings and at weekends, which makes the work faster.

In buildings with a shared entrance and a limited loading bay we book a slot with the property owner in advance. If a whole floor is being cleared we plan the order room by room so walkways stay clear. If you need to clear in stages – one open-plan area at a time, for example – that works well.
