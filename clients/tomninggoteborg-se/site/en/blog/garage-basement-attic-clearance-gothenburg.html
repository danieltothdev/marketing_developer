Guide · Secondary spaces

Clearing garages, basements, attics and storage in Gothenburg

The spaces that fill up first and get cleared last. Here we cover what each space needs, what counts as hazardous waste and what the clearance costs.

Garages, basements, attics and storage rooms are the spaces that fill up first and get cleared last. A garage or attic clearance in Gothenburg usually takes half a day to a full day and starts at around SEK 4,000, depending on the volume and how hard it is to carry things out.

What makes these spaces special is not the amount – it is the contents. A garage holds oils, paint tins and tyres. An attic holds insulation, building leftovers and furniture nobody could face carrying down. A basement often has damp, chemicals and old white goods. All of it needs different handling, and some of it must not be thrown out as ordinary waste. This guide covers what applies to each space.

Garage

Garage clearance in Gothenburg

A garage is rarely just a garage. After ten years it is a workshop, a storeroom and an overflow space all at once. In a garage clearance we typically meet car parts, tyres, tools, bicycles, building materials, garden machinery, paint and engine oil.

The practical side is usually simple – the garage is at ground level and the truck can park close by. What needs care is the sorting: tyres, waste oil, batteries, paint and solvents count as hazardous waste and must go to a dedicated facility. We handle that as part of the job, so you avoid several trips to the recycling centre yourself.

If you have a lawnmower, chainsaw or other machine with fuel still in the tank, let us know and we will drain it before transport. Read more about our garage and basement clearance service.

Basement

Basement clearance – damp, chemicals and heavy lifting

The basement is the space that most often holds surprises. Here you find the workshop, the preserves, old furniture that "might come in handy" and, not infrequently, damp damage meaning some of the contents must be discarded straight away.

In a basement clearance we pay particular attention to three things: the stairs (narrow, steep basement stairs mean heavy furniture sometimes has to be dismantled), the damp (furniture and textiles with mould are handled separately for health and safety reasons) and the chemicals (paint, thinner, pesticides and old medicines that must go to the right facility).

Old white goods in the basement – chest freezers, washing machines, tumble dryers – count as electrical waste and are collected separately. We have the equipment to get heavy items out of cramped basements without damaging walls or stairs.

Attic

Attic clearance – the hardest space to reach

An attic clearance is almost always more work than expected. Not because there is so much up there, but because everything has to be carried down a staircase rarely built for moving furniture – sometimes a hatch ladder with limited headroom.

In attics we typically find old suitcases and trunks, Christmas decorations and seasonal items, children’s things kept for later, furniture replaced twenty years ago, insulation offcuts and building materials from the last renovation. In older Gothenburg attics there may also be old insulation needing special handling – we assess that on site.

We bring extra staff when the attic is hard to access, precisely so the work is safe and quick. If the attic is the only part to be cleared, you can order just that – see storage clearance, which also covers attics.

Storage

Storage and self-storage unit clearance

A storage space can be many things: the unit in an apartment block’s basement, a free-standing shed by the house, or a rented self-storage unit. The need is usually the same – the space must be empty by a set date, often because the rental agreement is ending.

For self-storage clearance, time is usually what matters most. If the agreement ends at the turn of the month, the unit must be empty and swept or the rent keeps running. We work to that date and can prioritise the job if needed.

If you live in a housing association and it concerns shared spaces – bike rooms, pram rooms or basement corridors that have collected junk over the years – we help the association with bulk waste and shared areas.

Hazardous waste

What counts as hazardous waste?

The following must not go in ordinary bins and has to be taken to a dedicated facility. We handle all of it as part of the clearance:

  • Paint, varnish, glue, thinner and solvents – including dried-out tins.
  • Engine oil, waste oil and antifreeze, plus oil filters and rags that have been in contact with oil.
  • Car batteries and household batteries, including rechargeable ones.
  • Tyres – with or without rims.
  • Pesticides and chemicals from the garden and hobby use.
  • Fluorescent tubes, low-energy bulbs and electronics – classed as electrical waste.
  • White goods – fridges and freezers contain refrigerant and need special handling.
  • Old medicines – return to a pharmacy, never in the bin.

Sorting wrongly costs both the environment and money: incorrectly sorted waste at the recycling centre can incur fees. That is one reason many people let us take the whole handling.

Price

What does a garage, basement or attic clearance cost?

Prices normally start around SEK 4,000 for a smaller storage room or a half-full garage and rise with volume. Three things matter most: how much needs to go, how far it must be carried to the vehicle, and how much hazardous waste needs separate handling.

You get a fixed price after a free assessment – on site or from photos. Do send a few photos of the space when you request a quote; the assessment will be quicker and more accurate. See also our pricing overview and the guide to what a clearance costs.
